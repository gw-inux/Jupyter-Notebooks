**Mehl (2006), Figures 5 and 6 — piecewise case with ET.** The paper's conductivity numbers are $K_L=100$, $K_C=1$ and $K_R=0.1$; $h_L=2$ m and $h_R=0.5$ m. Interpret the conductivity numbers as m/day and choose spacing 100 m and width 1 m for this lab’s SI representation. Scale the paper’s ET discharge number 2 by the same factor as its conductances, giving a maximum positive loss $Q_{\mathrm{ET,max}}=2/(86400\cdot100)$ m³/s. Over the 100 m² central cell this is equivalent to 73.05 mm/year. The ET surface is SURF = 0.50 m and the extinction depth is EXDP = 0.25 m, giving an extinction elevation of 0.25 m. Recharge is zero. The true central head is approximately **0.415 m**.

Picard starts at **0.20 m**; Newton starts at **1.03 m**. The paths show accepted updates through convergence; segments beyond the common 0–2 m window are clipped visually only. The ET discharge is continuous, but its slope changes at the extinction and surface elevations. Because Picard incorporates the active slope in its linear equation, $f_P(h)$ can have jumps at those elevations. The plotted branches are deliberately disconnected; Newton's residual $f_N(h)$ remains continuous with corners.

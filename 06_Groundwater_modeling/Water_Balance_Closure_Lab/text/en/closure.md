Both methods seek the head that satisfies the same water balance. The flow residual measures how well water is conserved, and the head change measures how far the iteration moved. A solver can check both criteria. Changing homogeneous $K$ scales the flows but preserves the equilibrium head and the iteration path.

The three-cell exercise follows the teaching approach of [Mehl (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1745-6584.2006.00207.x). The Newton formulation is described by [Niswonger, Panday and Ibaraki (2011)](https://doi.org/10.3133/tm6A37).

The grain framework makes the water follow narrow, winding paths. This **resistance to flow** means a small inland water-level buildup can sustain flow toward the sea while recharge replenishes it.

Water moves from **higher to lower hydraulic head**: think of head as the level water would reach in a small observation tube. In this shallow example, the water table slopes gently toward the sea. “Downhill” means downhill in head, not always downward through the ground: groundwater can rise as it enters a river. The sea is a receiving boundary here, not a one-way connection everywhere. [S5, S13]

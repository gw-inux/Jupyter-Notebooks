The underground is not one solid block. It contains different materials: many deposits are made of loose or compacted grains, such as **sand and gravel**; elsewhere there is solid rock. A handful of sand helps us understand one common setting. The grains touch, but small spaces remain between them.

In sand, groundwater occupies **pores between grains**. It usually does not form a large underground lake. Groundwater also occurs in rock fractures and, in some rocks, caves; this activity focuses on sand.

A **porous medium** contains solid material and pore spaces. **Porosity** is pore volume divided by total volume (solids + pores). An **aquifer** can store and transmit useful quantities of groundwater. Connected pores permit movement, but porosity alone does not tell us how easily water flows. [S3, S4]

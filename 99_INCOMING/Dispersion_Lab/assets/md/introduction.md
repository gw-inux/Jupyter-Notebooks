One pathway tells us where one portion of solute travels. Many pathways reveal how a solute pulse spreads.

This laboratory starts with a **Galton board**: at each pin, a marker takes the left or right route with equal probability. You can then introduce different travel times or explore an idealized network between grey grains.

**After exploring, you should be able to:**

- distinguish transverse spreading, longitudinal separation, and variation in arrival times;
- explain why more markers give a clearer distribution without changing the transport mechanism;
- separate the effect of injection duration from the effect of transport;
- describe how pore openings influence flow and particle routing in the grain network.

**Try this first:** keep the regular board and an instantaneous pulse, select **Run highlighted particles**, then **Final result**. Next choose **Pinboard · variable travel times** and run again. What changes in the two outlet distributions?

Each marker represents an equal fraction of dissolved solute mass, not a solid fragment. Downward motion on the screen represents the main transport direction. Gravity drives the physical pinboard analogue; groundwater carries dissolved solutes in the corresponding transport setting.

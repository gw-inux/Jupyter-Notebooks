This section focuses on pumping tests in unconfined aquifers and the interpretation of drawdown derivatives using the Neuman solution. The Neuman solution extends the classical Theis approach by accounting for delayed drainage from the water table, which produces characteristic drawdown and derivative responses that are commonly observed in unconfined aquifers.

#### 💡 Motivation - Why study the Neuman solution and its derivative?

Many groundwater systems are unconfined and respond differently to pumping than confined aquifers. At early time, the aquifer behaves similarly to a confined system and drawdown is mainly controlled by elastic storage. At later time, water-table drainage becomes important and influences both drawdown and derivative behavior. Understanding these transitions is essential for interpreting pumping tests in unconfined aquifers and for distinguishing water-table effects from other hydrogeological processes.

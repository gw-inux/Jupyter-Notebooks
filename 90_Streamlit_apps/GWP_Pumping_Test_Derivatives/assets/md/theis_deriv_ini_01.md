This section introduces drawdown derivatives as a diagnostic tool for pumping test evaluation. The starting point is the well-known Theis solution for transient radial flow to a pumping well in an ideal confined aquifer. The Theis solution describes how drawdown evolves with time in response to pumping.

#### 💡 Motivation - Why are derivatives useful?

The drawdown curve shows the magnitude of aquifer response, whereas the derivative highlights changes in flow behavior. This additional information often makes it easier to identify characteristic flow regimes and recognize the influence of hydraulic boundaries. The derivative can be considered an additional magnifying glass that allows us to see important details in pumping-test data that may be difficult to recognize from the drawdown curve alone.


#### 🎯 Learning objectives

After completing this section, you should be able to:

- Explain the concept of a drawdown derivative.
- Relate the derivative to the Theis drawdown curve.
- Recognize the characteristic derivative plateau of radial flow.
- Identify deviations from ideal Theis behavior caused by hydraulic boundaries.

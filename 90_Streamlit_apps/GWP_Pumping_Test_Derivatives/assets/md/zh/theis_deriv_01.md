This section focuses on pumping tests in confined aquifers and the interpretation of drawdown derivatives using the Theis solution. The Theis solution describes transient radial flow to a pumping well and forms the foundation for many pumping-test evaluation methods.

#### 💡 Motivation - Why study the Theis solution and its derivative?

The Theis solution represents the simplest conceptual model of a pumping test and provides an important reference for interpreting field data. Understanding how transmissivity and storativity influence drawdown and derivative responses is essential before investigating more complex aquifer systems.

#### 🎯 Learning objectives

After completing this section, you should be able to:

- Describe how transmissivity influences drawdown and derivative responses.
- Describe how storativity influences drawdown and derivative responses.
- Recognize the characteristic derivative plateau of radial flow.
- Estimate transmissivity and storativity from pumping-test data.

#### 💧 How will we achieve this?

The section combines analytical solutions and synthetic pumping-test data.

1. Explore the influence of transmissivity on drawdown and derivatives.
2. Explore the influence of storativity on drawdown and derivatives.
3. Fit a Theis solution to pumping-test data and estimate aquifer parameters.

The examples are based on synthetic pumping-test data generated with analytical and numerical groundwater-flow models.

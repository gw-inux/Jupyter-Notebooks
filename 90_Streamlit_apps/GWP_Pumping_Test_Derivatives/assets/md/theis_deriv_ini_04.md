A drawdown derivative describes how rapidly drawdown changes with time. Rather than focusing on the magnitude of drawdown, the derivative highlights changes in aquifer response and often reveals important information that is difficult to recognize from drawdown data alone.

In pumping-test analysis, derivatives are typically computed with respect to the logarithm of time:

$$
d = \frac{\partial s}{\partial \ln(t)}
$$

Using logarithmic time emphasizes both early- and late-time behavior and allows characteristic flow regimes to be identified more easily.

When drawdown and derivative curves are interpreted together, it becomes possible to distinguish different aquifer responses, identify the influence of hydraulic boundaries, and improve parameter estimation. For this reason, drawdown derivatives have become a standard tool in modern pumping-test evaluation.

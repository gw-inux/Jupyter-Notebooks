Different values of interfacial tension and wettability can occur as a result of repeated drainage and imbibition of a soil. These pore-scale processes can result in different retention curves for the same soil. A soil that begins as fully saturated and undergoes drainage will exhibit a different drainage retention curve than that same soil if it starts completely dry and is then filled to saturation. These two retention curves define the largest range of variation between retention curves. At any point during filling or draining, the situation may change such that filling begins before drainage ends or vice versa and the retention curve will take a different shape between these end members. As a result, we can observe different water contents for the same pressure value in the same soil, depending on the history of filling and draining. This phenomenon is known as **hysteresis**.
